I actually think this is the **single most important answer** you'll give in the interview.

Not because it's the hardest.

Because it sets the tone for everything that follows.

Most candidates answer:

> "I've been in IT for 30 years..."

I don't think that's the right approach.

Instead, I want to answer the interviewer's _real_ question:

> **"Why should we spend the next hour talking to you?"**

---

# Story 0 — Tell Me About Yourself / Why SPG?

This is not a STAR story.

It's a narrative.

Think of it as:

**Past → Present → Future**

---

# Part 1 — My Background

> I've spent most of my career working at the intersection of business and technology, primarily in financial services and enterprise software implementations. My roles have been a blend of Business Analysis, implementation, testing, release coordination, and production support, where I've worked closely with business users, developers, QA, infrastructure, and operations teams to deliver complex enterprise systems.

Notice

I never said

"I'm a Business Analyst."

I described what you actually do.

---

# Part 2 — FRBNY

> Most recently I supported the Federal Reserve Bank of New York on the MRPrice platform, which distributes streaming fixed-income market data used by downstream trading and auction systems. I worked on requirements, acceptance criteria, testing, release readiness, API modernization, deployment coordination, troubleshooting, and production support while partnering closely with development and operations teams.

Notice

This isn't your resume.

It's your story.

---

# Part 3 — Why This Role

This is the important transition.

> As I started learning about Surefyre and the Technical Consultant role, I realized that although the business domain is different, the implementation lifecycle feels very familiar to me. Instead of configuring a financial platform, I'd be configuring an insurance platform. Instead of market data workflows, I'd be supporting underwriting workflows. But the work itself—understanding customer requirements, configuring solutions, validating integrations, testing, troubleshooting, documenting, and supporting production—is very much aligned with what I've been doing throughout my career.

That's the bridge.

---

# Part 4 — Why Insurance?

This is where most candidates struggle.

I wouldn't say:

> "Insurance seems interesting."

I'd say something like this.

> I also like that this role is customer-facing. Throughout my career I've enjoyed learning how different businesses operate and then helping translate those business processes into technology solutions. During the past week I've been learning much more about specialty insurance, MGAs, underwriting, and the Surefyre platform, and I find that combination of business process, configuration, and enterprise implementation genuinely interesting.

Notice

You're demonstrating curiosity.

Interviewers love that.

---

# Part 5 — Why SPG?

This is where you differentiate yourself.

> What really attracted me to SPG is that you're looking for someone who can bridge business and technology rather than being purely a software developer. The role combines business analysis, configuration, APIs, testing, troubleshooting, documentation, and customer interaction. Those are the areas where I've built the strongest experience over my career, and I think that's where I can contribute while continuing to grow technically.

---

# Closing

I would finish with:

> I know there will be a learning curve around Surefyre itself and some insurance-specific terminology. I'm comfortable with that. Throughout my career I've joined complex enterprise environments, learned the business, built relationships with technical and business teams, and become productive. That's exactly how I'd approach this opportunity.

---

## One refinement

After spending the last day building your preparation, I've noticed something that I think should become your **overall interview strategy**.

Don't try to convince them you're a **JavaScript developer**.

Don't try to convince them you're an **insurance expert**.

Instead, convince them of this:

> **"I'm an experienced enterprise implementation professional who learns business domains quickly, communicates well with customers and technical teams, and knows how to deliver reliable, well-tested production solutions."**

I believe that's the message that best matches both your actual experience and what SPG appears to be looking for.
