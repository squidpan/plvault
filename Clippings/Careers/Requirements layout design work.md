## Genesis Analysis — The BibleProject Method

Genesis confirms your original instinct: **the one-pager is the highest-level synthesis of extensive analysis, but the video is the guided path that teaches the viewer how to read that synthesis.**

The poster and presentation are one integrated product:

- The **poster** preserves the complete structure.
- The **video** controls attention and reveals the structure progressively.
- The **transcript** supplies the reasoning that connects the visual scenes.
- The **ending** explains what the accumulated picture means as a whole.

## 1\. The poster’s master architecture

The poster is not simply arranged from Genesis 1 through Genesis 50. It has several simultaneous layers.

| Visual layer | Genesis implementation | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Master title | **GENESIS** | Defines the subject |
| Governing thesis | God’s plan to rescue and bless his rebellious world through Abraham’s family | Explains why everything on the page belongs |
| Major acts | **1–11: God and the World** / **12–50: God and Abraham’s Family** | Divides the complexity into two comprehensible movements |
| Central hinge | Genesis 12:1–3—land, nation, name, blessing | Converts the problem into a response |
| Local episodes | Creation, rebellion, flood, Babylon, Abraham, Jacob, Joseph | Makes each portion understandable |
| Recurring patterns | Blessing, failure, deception, violence, rescue, faithfulness | Connects separated events conceptually |
| Final synthesis | Judah’s future king and restored worldwide blessing | Reconnects the ending to the original purpose |
| Open dependency | The family remains in Egypt; promises remain incomplete | Creates the reason to continue into Exodus |

## 2\. The layout itself carries meaning

The two halves are visually different because the nature of the information is different.

### Genesis 1–11: broad, organic, and interconnected

The left side concerns the whole world. It is presented as a widening downward spiral:

- Good creation
- Human choice
- Broken relationships
- Cain and Abel
- Violent civilization
- Flood
- Noah’s repeated failure
- Babylon
- Scattering

The large looping lines are not decorative. They communicate **recurrence and escalation**. Later events are larger-scale manifestations of the original problem.

The design is saying:

> These are not unrelated incidents. They are repeated instances of the same underlying human choice, producing increasingly extensive consequences.

That is essentially **root-cause analysis represented visually**.

### Genesis 12–50: narrower and more structured

The right side shifts into vertical generational panels:

- Abraham
- Isaac and Jacob
- Jacob’s sons
- Joseph

This reflects the narrative zoom:

> The scope narrows from the whole world to one family, but the purpose remains worldwide.

The structure becomes more orderly, but the family’s behavior remains disorderly. The page therefore preserves an important tension:

- God’s plan progresses.
- Human failure continues.
- The plan survives despite the participants repeatedly endangering it.

## 3\. The hinge is the key design device

Genesis 12:1–3 sits physically and conceptually between the two halves.

It answers the question created by Genesis 1–11:

> If humans repeatedly ruin the good world they were given, what will God do?

The answer is not yet the full solution. It is a strategic promise:

- Land
- Nation
- Great name
- Blessing
- Blessing extended to all families of the earth

This hinge performs four jobs simultaneously:

1. **Responds to the preceding problem.**
2. **Introduces the second half.**
3. **Connects backward to earlier themes.**
4. **Establishes the purpose of everything that follows.**

The transcript explicitly contrasts Abraham with Babylon:

- Babylon attempts to **make a great name** through its own power.
- God promises to **give Abraham a great name**.
- The original blessing given to humanity is now carried through Abraham’s family.

So the hinge is not just a midpoint. It is a **requirements traceability junction**.

## 4\. How the video progressively constructs the one-pager

### Part 1: Genesis 1–11

| Time | Narrative action | Visual-design purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 00:00–00:33 | Establishes the two-part structure and hinge | Gives viewers the map before the details |
| 00:33–01:58 | Introduces the good world, humans, purpose, blessing, and choice | Defines baseline state and governing principles |
| 01:59–04:25 | Introduces rebellion and its immediate consequences | Establishes the primary problem and causal chain |
| 04:26–06:43 | Shows recurring failure at increasing scale | Demonstrates pattern, escalation, and systemic impact |
| 06:44–07:42 | Interprets all the stories as one argument | Converts accumulated details into meaning |
| Ending | Asks what God will do | Creates the transition into the hinge |

Notice the presentation sequence:

> Baseline → choice → failure → consequences → repeated pattern → interpretation → unresolved question

The speaker does not merely narrate events. After accumulating the evidence, he pauses and says, in effect:

> These diverse stories are all exploring the same basic point.

That sentence is the analytical synthesis.

### Part 2: Genesis 12–50

| Time | Narrative action | Visual-design purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 00:00–00:31 | Recaps the entire problem from Part 1 | Restores context before continuing |
| 00:32–02:20 | Introduces Abraham and the promise | Reveals the central hinge and governing solution |
| 02:21–04:20 | Defines the two repeating themes | Gives viewers a framework for interpreting all remaining episodes |
| 04:20–05:18 | Shows the pattern intensifying through Jacob | Demonstrates repetition with variation |
| 05:19–07:48 | Brings every major theme together in Joseph and Judah | Converges the narrative |
| 07:49–08:08 | Identifies the unresolved future promises | Ends with both synthesis and forward dependency |

The two repeating themes are:

1. Abraham’s family repeatedly fails and jeopardizes the promise.
2. God remains faithful and preserves the promise.

Once those themes are stated, the audience no longer receives the remaining stories as isolated events. They know what pattern to watch for.

That is an important lesson for requirements presentation:

> **Give stakeholders an interpretive framework before showing them large amounts of detail.**

## 5\. The ending changes the meaning of everything before it

Joseph’s statement—his brothers intended evil, but God redirected it toward saving lives—is strategically placed near the end.

It works at three levels:

- Explains Joseph’s individual experience.
- Summarizes the repeated family pattern.
- Reinterprets the whole book from Genesis 3 onward.

Then Jacob’s blessing of Judah reaches even farther back:

- The promised king comes from Judah.
- He will command the nations.
- He will restore the worldwide blessing.
- This reconnects to the original good world and the promised defeat of evil.

Therefore, the ending is not just “what happened last.” It is the **final synthesis lens** through which the viewer re-reads the entire poster.

And yet Genesis ends with the family in Egypt and the promises incomplete. The ending performs two seemingly opposite tasks:

- It provides conceptual closure.
- It preserves operational incompleteness.

That creates the natural transition into Exodus.

## 7\. A first draft of our master requirements composition

A BibleProject-inspired requirements one-pager could use this architecture:

### Top: governing meaning

- Initiative name
- One-sentence transformation thesis
- Business purpose
- Two or three persistent themes

### Left: the world before the change

- Original operating intent
- Current actors and systems
- Triggering limitation
- Repeated failure patterns
- Business and regulatory consequences
- Why localized fixes have failed

### Center: the hinge

- The strategic decision
- The pivotal capability
- The non-negotiable business outcome
- The requirement that changes the direction of the story

### Right: the transformation

- Capabilities or workstreams
- System and data changes
- Release progression
- Exception handling
- Controls
- Stakeholder outcomes

### Ending: synthesis and value

- How the proposed state resolves the opening tension
- How major requirements connect
- What value is restored or created
- What remains for later releases
- Links to detailed supporting artifacts

## 8\. The most important conclusion

The Genesis method is not:

> Put every requirement on one page.

It is:

> Discover the few governing ideas that make hundreds of details intelligible, organize the details around those ideas, and reveal the structure in an order that allows stakeholders to understand why everything connects.

The final one-pager must preserve four kinds of understanding simultaneously:

1. **Sequence:** What happens and in what order?
2. **Causality:** Why does one condition produce another?
3. **Pattern:** What repeats across different parts of the system?
4. **Purpose:** How does the ending resolve the problem introduced at the beginning?

That is the first foundational principle of our method:

> **A requirements one-pager is not a compressed requirements document. It is a visual argument explaining the design of the change.**
