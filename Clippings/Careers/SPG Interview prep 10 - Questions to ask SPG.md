This is one of the most overlooked parts of interview preparation.

At the end of the interview, almost everyone asks:

> "What does a typical day look like?"

or

> "What's the company culture like?"

Those are fine, but they don't distinguish you.

I want your questions to accomplish **three goals**:

1. Show you've researched the role.
    
2. Show you think like an implementation consultant.
    
3. Help you decide whether you'll enjoy the job.
    

You probably won't ask all of these. Pick **5–8** depending on how much time is left.

---

# Category 1 — The Role (Ask These First)

## 1. What does success look like during the first 90 days?

**Why it's good**

It tells them you're already thinking about becoming productive.

**Good follow-up**

> What would you hope I'd be able to do independently by that point?

---

## 2. What separates your strongest Technical Consultants from average ones?

This is one of my favorites.

It tells you what they actually value.

Listen for things like:

- customer communication
    
- ownership
    
- troubleshooting
    
- configuration
    
- documentation
    
- technical depth
    

---

## 3. Where do new consultants typically spend the most time learning?

Good follow-up:

> Is it the insurance domain, Surefyre itself, or customer-specific implementations?

That naturally acknowledges you're learning without making it a weakness.

---

# Category 2 — Customer Implementations

## 4. What does a typical Surefyre implementation look like from kickoff through go-live?

This question shows you've been thinking about the implementation lifecycle.

Possible follow-up:

- How long is a typical project?
    
- How many consultants are usually involved?
    
- Is one consultant assigned end-to-end?
    

---

## 5. Which phase of an implementation tends to be the most challenging?

Excellent question.

You may hear:

- requirements
    
- configuration
    
- customer change requests
    
- integrations
    
- data migration
    
- UAT
    

That tells you where the real work is.

---

## 6. How much of the consultant's time is spent on new implementations versus supporting existing customers?

Very useful.

You want to know if the role is:

- 90% new implementations
    

or

- mostly production support.
    

---

# Category 3 — Configuration

## 7. How much configuration is done through the Surefyre platform versus custom development by Engineering?

I really like this one.

It tells you:

What does the Technical Consultant actually configure?

What gets handed to Engineering?

---

## 8. How are configuration changes typically managed?

Example follow-up:

> Do consultants configure directly in development environments and then promote changes through QA and Production?

This ties directly to your release management experience.

---

# Category 4 — Technology

## 9. How much JavaScript do consultants typically write?

This is a great question because it helps calibrate expectations.

Possible answers:

Small snippets

Large customization

Mostly reading existing code

Configuration only

---

## 10. What are the most common integrations consultants work with?

Could be:

- AIM
    
- ImageRight
    
- carrier systems
    
- rating engines
    
- document management
    
- payment platforms
    
- identity providers
    

This is a good technical discussion starter.

---

## 11. How do Technical Consultants typically use Python?

I really like this.

Maybe it's used for:

- migration
    
- imports
    
- exports
    
- validation
    
- automation
    

Now you'll know exactly how much Python matters.

---

# Category 5 — Customers

## 12. Who are your typical customers?

This is almost a free question.

Are they mostly:

- MGAs?
    
- carriers?
    
- wholesalers?
    
- brokers?
    
- program administrators?
    

You learn a lot.

---

## 13. Do consultants typically stay with one customer for an entire implementation?

Great consulting question.

---

# Category 6 — Team

## 14. How does Professional Services interact with Product and Engineering?

I love this question.

It demonstrates you've already thought about cross-functional work.

---

## 15. When a customer requests something that requires product changes rather than configuration, how does that process usually work?

This is exactly the type of question an experienced implementation consultant asks.

---

# Category 7 — Growth

## 16. Where do successful consultants usually grow after a few years?

Could be:

- Senior Consultant
    
- Solution Architect
    
- Product
    
- Customer Success
    
- Implementation Manager
    

Shows you're thinking long term.

# My Top 10 Questions

If I only had time for ten:

1. What does success look like during the first 90 days?
    
2. What separates your strongest Technical Consultants from average ones?
    
3. What does a typical implementation lifecycle look like?
    
4. Which phase of implementation is usually the most challenging?
    
5. How much configuration is platform configuration versus custom development?
    
6. How much JavaScript do consultants typically write?
    
7. How are configuration changes promoted through environments?
    
8. How does Professional Services partner with Product and Engineering?
    
9. What types of customers would I most likely work with?
    
10. Where do successful consultants usually grow within SPG?

# Questions I Would Ask This Team

Based on the job description and what we've learned about Surefyre, these are the ones I think fit best:

### Toward the Hiring Manager

> As someone coming from enterprise financial systems rather than insurance platforms, where do you think the biggest learning curve will be for a new Technical Consultant?

This is a confident way to address your transition while inviting useful insight.

---

### Toward the Technical Interviewer

> We've talked a lot about configuration, integrations, and troubleshooting. Looking at your current customer implementations, which of those areas tends to consume the most consultant time?

That invites them to describe the realities of the job.

---

### Toward the Team

> If we were sitting here a year from now and you said, "Paul has been a great hire," what would I have accomplished that made you feel that way?

This is my favorite closing question.

It changes the conversation from your past to your future.

---

# Questions to Avoid

I would avoid these in the first interview unless they raise them first:

- Vacation policy
    
- Work-from-home flexibility (they've already said hybrid)
    
- Salary negotiation
    
- Bonus details
    
- Overtime expectations
    
- Promotions
    
- "How soon can I work remotely?"
    

Those are appropriate later, after they've decided you're the person they want.

---

# My Favorite Closing

If the interview has gone well, I would finish with something like this:

> "Thank you. I really appreciate the conversation. The more I've learned about the role, the more I think it's a good fit for the type of work I enjoy—understanding business processes, implementing enterprise solutions, and helping customers be successful. I know I'd have a learning curve with Surefyre itself, but I feel the implementation lifecycle aligns very well with my experience, and I'm excited about the opportunity."

It's confident, honest, and reinforces the central message we've built throughout your preparation:

> **You're not selling yourself as a Surefyre expert. You're selling yourself as an experienced enterprise implementation professional who can quickly learn the platform and help customers succeed.**
