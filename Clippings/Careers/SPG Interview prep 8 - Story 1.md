# Story 1 — MRPrice Modernization (Implementation and Modernization)

## Why this story?

This is the story you'll probably use the most.

It answers questions like:

- Tell me about your most significant project.
    
- Tell me about a complex implementation.
    
- Tell me about modernization.
    
- Tell me about working with developers.
    
- Tell me about changing business processes.
    
- Tell me about enterprise software.
    
- Tell me about APIs.
    
- Tell me about cross-functional work.
    

---

# Situation

When I joined the Federal Reserve Bank of New York project, the MRPrice platform was already a mission-critical system used to cleanse, validate, and distribute streaming fixed-income market data.

The organization had begun a multi-year modernization effort to move from a legacy on-premises architecture toward a more modern cloud-based platform using AWS and REST services, while maintaining uninterrupted support for downstream consumers.

---

# Challenge

The biggest challenge wasn't simply replacing technology.

It was modernizing a business-critical platform without disrupting operations.

That meant understanding:

- existing business workflows
    
- legacy data structures
    
- downstream consumers
    
- deployment processes
    
- testing requirements
    
- operational readiness
    

Every change had to preserve existing business behavior while enabling future capabilities.

---

# My Role

I served as a Senior Business Analyst and DevOps Release Coordinator embedded with the development team.

My responsibilities included:

- working with Product Owners and business users
    
- refining epics into Jira stories
    
- writing acceptance criteria
    
- supporting API modernization discussions
    
- validating business rules
    
- coordinating with developers
    
- supporting QA and UAT
    
- preparing releases
    
- validating production readiness
    
- helping troubleshoot production issues
    

Although I wasn't writing the Java services, I was deeply involved in ensuring the solution met business requirements and could be deployed successfully.

---

# Result

The modernization progressed while maintaining operational stability for systems that depended on streaming market data.

The project strengthened:

- release discipline
    
- testing
    
- API adoption
    
- operational readiness
    
- cross-team collaboration
    

---

# What I Learned

One thing I learned is that successful modernization is much more about people, business processes, and implementation discipline than technology alone.

Technology changes.

Good implementation practices don't.

---

One reason I'm excited about the SPG opportunity is that I see many similarities.

Those are areas where I've spent much of my career.

# Interviewer's Hidden Question

When they ask:

They're actually asking:

# If They Ask About Java

> **Interviewer:** "How much Java development did you do?"

I would answer:

> "I wasn't part of the Java development team. My role was translating business requirements, validating behavior, supporting testing, and coordinating implementation. I became comfortable reading technical artifacts and working closely with developers, but I wouldn't describe myself as a Java developer."

That answer is accurate and builds credibility.

---

# If They Ask About AWS

> **Interviewer:** "What was your AWS involvement?"

I would answer:

> "My involvement was from the implementation and operational side rather than cloud engineering. I needed to understand how the modernization affected deployments, environments, integrations, operational readiness, and downstream consumers so I could coordinate releases and validate successful implementation."

Again, you're positioning yourself exactly where you were—not overstating your hands-on cloud engineering.

## Interview Coaching

One suggestion for Story 1:

Don't recite it as a memorized speech.

Think of it as having five anchor points:

1. **The system** — MRPrice, mission-critical market-data platform.
    
2. **The challenge** — modernize without disrupting production.
    
3. **Your role** — BA, implementation, testing, release coordination, cross-functional collaboration.
    
4. **The outcome** — successful modernization with operational stability.
    
5. **The bridge** — explain why those implementation disciplines transfer directly to configuring and supporting Surefyre.
    

If you can naturally cover those five points, you'll answer a wide variety of questions with confidence while staying grounded in your actual experience.
